My role

Team

Making math feel familiar

We started with an everyday math problem. A customer orders a drink, pays, and waits for their change. We turned that interaction into a game for elementary students.

Designing the checkout

Our team sketched the cashier experience in Figma. A round gives the player an order and asks for the total or the change due. A short visual response lets the player see how they did before moving on.

Building the game

I led the project from the initial concept through the demo. We built the mobile game in Thunkable and presented it at ANova Hacks, where it earned 2nd place for Outstanding Java Project in a field of 20 teams.

Research / 2025

A multi-wavelength laser upgrade.

Beam diagnostics and calibration for a high-harmonic generation spectroscopy experiment at Berkeley Lab.

Before crystal
After crystal
Pump path
My role
Discovery Undergraduate Research Scholar
Team
Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory
Year

The research question

To study how a material responds to light, researchers need control over the light they send into it. Our Spring 2025 project added a 515 nm pump option to an optical system used for time-resolved photoemission spectroscopy.

Measuring the beam

I built Python and MATLAB tools to automate beam diagnostics and calibration. We characterized the beam before and after the frequency-doubling crystal and along the pump path, comparing the shape and intensity at each position.

What the team observed

Our measurements showed that pulse energy and alignment affected conversion efficiency. The project poster documents the optical setup, the beam profiles, and the measurements collected at different repetition rates.

Engineering / 2024

A soft robotic gripper.

Taking a liquid crystal elastomer gripper from a CAD concept to a heated, load-tested finger.

My role
Project lead and CAD design
Team
UC Berkeley senior capstone
Year

Designing for movement

A conventional gripper can use joints and motors to grasp an object. We printed a finger from a liquid crystal elastomer and used heat to make it bend.

Meet the gripper.

Rotate a simplified model of our CAD concept, then adjust the fingers to explore the proposed grasp. The load tests below used one printed finger.

20%

From the assembly to a testable part

I designed the gripper concept and the initial finger geometry in CAD, prepared the print slices, and worked on digital light processing printing. A single finger gave us a practical way to test how the material moved under load.

Testing the printed finger

We ran thermal and weight tests with the printed sample, including loads of 211.6 mg and 1,059.3 mg. We compared how far the finger bent under each load. Our design earned a top 3 finish in senior capstone design.

Product / 2026

Storage planning at IBM.

Turning GPU and capacity requirements into storage options that sales engineers can compare.

Storage Scale
My role
Technical product manager
Team
IBM Storage Solutions
Year
2026

The decision behind the tool

When a customer describes an AI workload, the sales engineer has to translate that conversation into hardware. GPU count, usable capacity, and read and write speeds all matter. A system with enough storage can still be too slow for the workload.

Three ways to start

I built a planning tool that starts with the information the engineer already has. They can enter a workload requirement, a GPU setup, or the hardware on hand. It brings server count, usable capacity, and estimated throughput into the same view so the engineer can compare the trade-offs.

What partner feedback changed

The first version gave engineers two drive configurations. A hardware partner wanted to model the options in between. I expanded support to five configurations and made it easier to see how a change in drive count affected the recommendation.
